# Red team: attempts to break the finding

**Claim under attack.** On the Saturdays and Sundays that are official working days only in
mainland China (make-up workdays, 调休), highly active GitHub repositories become measurably more
active than on ordinary weekends. The size of that jump gives a location-free estimate of how much
of a project's work runs on the mainland-China calendar. A smaller jump also appears in projects
with few or no Chinese-language issue and PR titles.

All numbers come from `results/*.json` (regenerated by `run_all.sh`; summary in `results/SUMMARY.md`).
E1 = 2023-01-15..2024-06-03, with 12 make-up days. E2 = 2025-10-16..2026-07-01, with 4 make-up days.
"s" is the calendar-swap share: 0 means the day looks like an ordinary weekend, 1 means it looks
like an ordinary weekday.

## Verdict

**The core finding survives.** The make-up-workday signal is large and specific to the calendar
(placebo ≈ 0) in repos with ≥1% Chinese titles: 0.652 [0.52, 0.77]. It tracks an independent text
signal, is absent in the one clean same-time-zone control we had (Japanese), and holds up across
thresholds, metrics, leave-one-out and holiday-proximity tests. It predicts which repos show it in
a later year (split-half, 0.581) and has the right sign two years later (E2, sign only).

**Survives with caveats.** The small aggregate share in repos with <1% Chinese titles, 0.062
[0.046, 0.082], is positive on 12 of 12 days and concentrated in Beijing office hours. But up to
about half of it may be holiday proximity (row 20), and its scale depends on the day boundary
(0.048 with UTC days; row 23).

**What does not survive or remains open.** E2 (2025–26) cannot replicate magnitudes (4 days,
capture loss): sign only. The Korean control is inconclusive and the Taiwan control is not
possible. Per-repo estimates are too noisy to publish. The holiday-dip mirror is uncalibrated.
Any reading of s as "share of contributors from China" depends on assumptions (rows 15, 17).
