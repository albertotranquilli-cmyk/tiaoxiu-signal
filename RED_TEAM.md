# Red team: attempts to break the finding

**Claim under attack.** On the Saturdays and Sundays that are official working days only in
mainland China (make-up workdays, 调休), highly active GitHub repositories become measurably more
active than on ordinary weekends. The size of that jump gives a location-free estimate of how much
of a project's work runs on the mainland-China calendar. The jump also appears in many projects
whose issue and PR titles are almost entirely in English.

All numbers come from `results/*.json` (regenerated by `run_all.sh`; summary in `results/SUMMARY.md`).
E1 = 2023-01-15..2024-06-03, with 12 make-up days. E2 = 2025-10-16..2026-07-01, with 4 make-up days.
"s" is the calendar-swap share: 0 means the day looks like an ordinary weekend, 1 means it looks
like an ordinary weekday.

| # | Attack | Test | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Base rate / chance.** Any weekend day might show "uplift" against a noisy baseline. | Placebo: the same estimator on 500 sets of ordinary weekend days, matched on weekday. | Survives. Placebo mean is about 0 in every group (for example +0.001 ± 0.012 for the <1%-Chinese group). Real values: 0.652 (≥1% Chinese titles, z≈55) and 0.062 (<1% Chinese titles, z≈5). Permutation p < 0.002 in both. |
| 2 | **Time-zone artifact.** Days are cut at UTC+8 midnight, so Asian-time-zone repos might look "different" on weekends. | (a) Placebo weekends use the same UTC+8 cut and show nothing. (b) Same-time-zone negative control: repos with mostly Japanese titles (UTC+9; Japan has no make-up days). | Survives, but control (b) is small. Japanese repos: s = −0.032 [−0.10, 0.04], n=14. Simplified-Chinese control panel: 0.739 [0.70, 0.79], n=283. |
| 3 | **Korean control.** Korea is UTC+9 with no make-up days, so s should be about 0. | Same as 2b. | **Inconclusive.** Pooled s=0.27 with CI [−0.04, 0.83]; placebo sd is 0.24, so not significant. Korean repos at this activity level are dominated by coding-bootcamp repos with class schedules (median per-repo s ≈ 0.06). Not a clean control. |
| 4 | **Taiwan/Hong Kong control** (UTC+8, Chinese script, different calendar). | Traditional-character-dominant repos. | **Not testable.** Fewer than 5 such repos meet the activity threshold. Open limitation. |
| 5 | **A global event on those dates** (Hacktoberfest starts 1 Oct, releases, conferences). | Leave-one-event-out; per-event estimates; drop 189 campaign, course and tutorial repos. | Survives. LOO range for <1%-Chinese repos is 0.058–0.066; for ≥1%-Chinese repos 0.629–0.690. The <1% group is positive on **12 of 12** days (sign test p = 0.0002). Dropping campaign/course repos gives 0.058 (z 5.3). |
| 6 | **Pre-/post-holiday rush, not work.** Make-up days sit next to Chinese holidays. | Saturday-only vs Sunday-only events; reference windows exclude ±2 days around every Chinese holiday. | Survives, with one note. The adjacency is itself a mainland-calendar effect, so it does not create a false "China" signal. Sat-only: 0.120 (<1%) and 0.632 (≥1%). Sun-only: 0.054 and 0.660. |
| 7 | **Threshold / window / metric fishing.** | Grid: reference half-window 14/21/28 days × min weekday–weekend gap 5/10/20/40; distinct actors vs raw event counts. | Survives. <1% group 0.061–0.071 across all 12 cells; ≥1% group 0.644–0.666; Apache 0.331–0.354. Event counts: 0.047 / 0.755 / 0.358. |
| 8 | **Multiple comparisons at repo level.** | Per-repo z against that repo's own placebo distribution; Benjamini–Hochberg across 3,581 repos. | 289 repos at q<0.05, 159 of them with <1% Chinese titles. Per-repo results are noisy (see 12), so the holdouts below are what we rely on. |
| 9 | **Overfitting / no out-of-sample check.** | (a) Split-half: flag repos on the 7 make-up days of 2023, measure them on the 5 of 2024. (b) Era holdout: flag on E1, measure two years later on E2. | **Survives.** (a) 206 flagged in 2023 → s = 0.556 [0.50, 0.61] in 2024; the 106 with <1% Chinese titles → 0.414 [0.33, 0.50]; unflagged → 0.040. (b) 42 flagged in E1 → 0.623 [0.50, 0.73] in E2; the 22 English-only ones → 0.481 [0.31, 0.63]; unflagged → −0.026. |
| 10 | **Replication of the aggregate <1% number in E2.** | Same group in E2. | **Does not replicate at group level.** s = 0.017 [−0.007, 0.045], z = 0.2. E2 has only 4 usable make-up days, so placebo sd is 0.066 vs 0.012 in E1, and it covers a different, smaller repo set (614 eligible). We cannot tell low power from a real decline. Innovation Graph shows China's developer share falling from 9.0% to 5.9% over the same years. We do **not** claim a trend. |
| 11 | **Driven by a handful of repos.** | Remove the top 10/50/100 repos by contribution to the <1% numerator. | Partly concentrated: 0.062 → 0.053 → 0.038 → 0.027. Removing the largest contributors biases the result toward zero by construction, but it stays positive. Read as "a long tail plus a core of strongly China-calendar English-language projects". |
| 12 | **Per-repo noise.** Small weekday–weekend gaps make ratios explode (some s > 3). | — | Real limitation. We publish no per-repo rankings. Only pooled group and organisation estimates with bootstrap CIs. |
| 13 | **Leakage between the estimator and the validation signal.** | — | None. The Chinese-title share comes from issue/PR title text. s comes only from daily counts of distinct active accounts. Neither enters the other. |
| 14 | **The holiday mirror image should agree.** | China-only holiday weekdays (excluding Jan 1, May 1, Carnival, US federal holidays, Diwali, Eid). | Agrees qualitatively; not calibrated. Dip 0.04 for <1% Chinese vs 0.88–1.18 for Chinese-text groups. Values above 1 mean holidays are quieter than weekends. Per-repo rank correlation with s is weak (0.11 in E1). Before we excluded **Carnival and US Presidents' Day**, which overlap Spring Festival 2024/2026, non-Chinese repos showed a spurious 2026 "Spring Festival dip": 0.16 for unflagged repos in an earlier run, 0.07 after the exclusion. This is why holiday dips alone are a poor instrument and why we use make-up days. |
| 15 | **Equal-weekend-ratio assumption** (needed to read s as "share of weekday activity"). | Median weekend/weekday ratio by group. | Roughly holds: 0.37 (<1% Chinese) vs 0.32 (≥20% Chinese). Even mostly-Chinese repos reach only s ≈ 0.74, not 1. Make-up days are not full workdays for everyone (leave, hobby activity, non-mainland members). Treat s as a **conservative** share; dividing by about 0.74 gives a rough upper reading. |
| 16 | **Sampling bias.** | — | Limitation. Only the most active public repos (≥50 distinct human accounts and ≥5,000 human events in E1). GH Archive covers public events only. Bots are filtered by login name, which is imperfect, but bots do not follow the PRC calendar, so leftover bots push s toward 0. |
| 17 | **What s actually measures.** | — | Limitation. s measures people who keep the **mainland-China official work calendar**, not nationality. Chinese developers abroad are not counted; foreign staff in China, and Chinese firms that skip make-up days, blur it. |
| 18 | **Data gaps in the source.** | Days with fewer than 24 hours of data are dropped. | The ClickHouse playground copy of GH Archive has no data from 2024-06-05 to 2025-09-25 and almost none for 2025-10-09..14. That is why E2 is short and has only 4 make-up days. Loading the raw GH Archive files for the gap would add 6 more make-up days; not done here. |
| 19 | **External sanity check.** | GitHub-wide estimate vs Innovation Graph. | Weak, context only. All public GitHub (dominated by automation): s = 0.044 (p = 0.036) in E1, not significant in E2. This is not comparable to Innovation Graph's 8–9% (2023) developer share, and we use it as neither validation nor a claim. |

## Verdict

**The core finding survives.** The make-up-workday signal is large and specific to the calendar
(placebo ≈ 0). It tracks an independent text signal, is absent in the one clean same-time-zone
control we had (Japanese), and holds up across thresholds, metrics and leave-one-out. Most
importantly, it predicts which repos show it in a later year (split-half) and two years later
(E2).

**What does not survive or remains open.** The E2 replication of the small aggregate share for
English-only repos fails (underpowered). The Korean control is inconclusive and the Taiwan control
is not possible. Per-repo estimates are too noisy to publish. The holiday-dip mirror is
uncalibrated. Any reading of s as "share of contributors from China" depends on assumptions
(row 15, row 17).
