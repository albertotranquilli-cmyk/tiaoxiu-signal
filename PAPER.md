## Title

Make-up workdays as a natural experiment: measuring open-source calendar adherence without geolocation

## Abstract

Mainland China turns a few Saturdays and Sundays each year into official working days (调休, make-up workdays) to pay for longer holidays. No other country works those days; to time zones, seasonality and Western holidays they are ordinary weekends. That makes each one a natural experiment for measuring how much of a project's activity follows the mainland-China work calendar — without touching any personal data.

We define a simple estimator, s: the share of a repository's weekday-versus-weekend activity gap that switches on on make-up days. We run it on GH Archive over 12 make-up days in 2023–24 across ~3,600 of the most active public repositories. Repositories with Chinese-language issue titles run at s ≈ 0.65 [0.52, 0.77] (placebo ≈ 0). Repositories with <1% Chinese titles show a smaller but real effect: s ≈ 0.062 [0.046, 0.082], positive on 12 of 12 days, with 78% of the excess falling in Beijing office hours. Pooled over its 71 most active repositories, the Apache Software Foundation scores s ≈ 0.33 despite only 1.3% of its issue titles being in Chinese. Known positives: Baidu PaddlePaddle 0.59, Alibaba 0.59. Near zero: AWS −0.01, Mozilla 0.02.

The estimator uses only per-repository daily counts of distinct active accounts plus the official Chinese calendar. It never looks at a user. A full red-team file documents what did not survive: up to about half of the <1% signal may be holiday proximity, the 2025–26 window is sign-only due to GH Archive capture loss, and s measures calendar adherence, not nationality.

## 1. Introduction

Open-source risk and diversity analysis typically geolocates contributors via profile locations, e-mail suffixes or commit time-zone offsets (Wachs et al. 2022; Rossi & Zacchiroli 2022). That requires per-person attributes and raises privacy concerns. Holiday dips in GitHub activity are known (GitHub State of the Octoverse 2018; Shen 2020) but start from a known location and show that holidays matter — they do not go the other way, estimating a project's calendar composition from its activity alone.

Make-up workdays solve both problems at once: they are days that only mainland China works, they border no Western holiday, and they are invisible to any analysis that does not know the Chinese calendar. Each one is a clean natural experiment.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Data

GH Archive via the public ClickHouse playground. E1 = 2023-01-15..2024-06-03 (12 make-up days); E2 = 2025-10-16..2026-07-01 (4 make-up days, sign-only due to capture loss). Eligible set: repositories with ≥50 distinct human accounts and ≥5,000 human events in E1, ~3,600 repos. Bots filtered by login name.

### 2.2 Estimator

s = (activity on make-up day − activity on matched ordinary weekend) / (activity on matched ordinary weekday − activity on matched ordinary weekend). s = 0 means the day looks like an ordinary weekend; s = 1 means it looks like an ordinary weekday. Days cut at Asia/Shanghai midnight. Activity = distinct human accounts per day.

### 2.3 Validation

- Placebo: 500 sets of ordinary weekend days matched on weekday. Placebo mean ≈ 0 in every group.
- Negative control: repositories with mostly Japanese-language titles (UTC+9, Japan has no make-up days): s = −0.032 [−0.10, 0.04].
- Split-half: repos flagged on 2023 make-up days score s = 0.581 [0.47, 0.68] on 2024 days.
- Era holdout: E1-flagged repos are positive in E2 (sign only).
- Leave-one-event-out, threshold grid, two-way bootstrap CIs (repos × days), Benjamini–Hochberg across 3,581 repos.

## 4. Limitations

- s measures people who keep the mainland-China official work calendar, not nationality. Chinese developers abroad are not counted; foreign staff in China blur it.
- Up to about half of the <1% signal may be holiday proximity (difference-in-differences and partner-day placebos).
- E2 (2025–26) is sign-only: GH Archive capture degrades sharply from mid-2025 (PR events −57%, PR review comments −55%).
- The Korean control is inconclusive (bootcamp-dominated); a Taiwan control was not possible (<5 eligible repos).
- Per-repo estimates are too noisy to publish; only pooled group and organisation estimates.
- The scale of s for Western-heavy repos depends on the day boundary (0.048 with UTC days).
- Sampling: only the most active public repos; GH Archive covers public events only.
